# Fact-check and sources

Checked 2026-09-10. Status of the rulemaking on that date: proposed rule and
draft EIS published 2026-08-20 (91 FR 53827, RIN 0596-AD66, docket
FS-2025-0001); comments close 2026-09-21; no final rule; Idaho and Colorado
state rules explicitly unaffected; Tongass included in the national rescission.

## Claims the story makes, and how well they hold up

| Claim                                                                                    | Verdict                  | Basis                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                          |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| The 2001 rule covered 58.5 M acres; 44.7 M are on the table now                          | Supported                | 58.5 M at promulgation (66 FR 3244). Idaho (9.3 M, 2008) and Colorado (4.2 M, 2012) have state rules the proposal keeps. The proposed rule's own figure is 44.7 M; our USFS data subset sums to 44,701,002.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| Roadless areas hold intact, high-integrity forest and matter for water, wildlife, carbon | Supported                | Mildrexler et al., _Biological Conservation_ 321 (2026): IRAs have "among the highest integrity forests in the nation." Olden et al., _PLOS Water_ (2026): ~25 M people's surface drinking water traces to IRA-influenced watersheds. Dietz et al. 2021: IRAs add 29% more net primary productivity to the protected-area system. USDA DEIS: 327 T&E species likely adversely affected. Do **not** quote an old-growth percentage; none exists.                                                                |
| Local communities, tribes, hunters and anglers oppose the change                         | Supported, with care     | USDA: >220,000 letters on behalf of 625,737 people and organizations on the 2025 notice; Center for Western Priorities sample >99% opposed. Proposed rule text: "majority sentiment among Tribal governments consulted is opposition." BHA, Trout Unlimited, La Plata County CO, Buncombe County NC opposed. Say "overwhelming majority of commenters," not "the public." Note the National Association of Counties supports repeal.                                                                           |
| It benefits a small number of industry interests, not the public                         | Partly supported         | USDA's own numbers: at most $4.6 to $10.6 M/yr to the timber industry and $5.2 to $11.4 M/yr to the Treasury, "unlikely" to be reached; roads cost $80k to >$2 M per mile; $6.9 B road maintenance backlog. Tongass timber lost ~$1.73 B 1980 to 2019 (TCS). "Only a few rich people" is rhetorical and unsupported; the story instead names the beneficiaries (regional mills, contractors, mineral and energy leaseholders).                                                                                 |
| Tourism and recreation are the bigger economy                                            | Supported                | BEA ORSA 2024: $696.7 B value added, 2.4% of GDP, 5.2 M jobs. NVUM: 158.7 M national forest visits/yr. Southeast Alaska 2024: visitor industry 8,589 jobs vs timber 274 (Southeast Conference).                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| It will not noticeably lower lumber prices                                               | Supported by inference   | Federal forests supply ~4% of US timber (RFF 2025). USDA: 5 to 10% more NFS sawtimber at most. Canada ~24% of US consumption; AD/CVD ~35% plus 10% Section 232 tariff since Oct 2025. Fastmarkets: offsetting Canada would need a 450% increase in federal harvest. No named economist has said this about roadless specifically; the story builds it from the numbers.                                                                                                                                        |
| Roads increase fire ignitions; roadless areas are not the fire problem                   | Supported, with a caveat | Aplet, Hartger and Dietz, _Fire Ecology_ (2026): 7.99 ignitions per 1,000 ha within 50 m of roads vs 1.97 in IRAs (authors are Wilderness Society scientists). Kilbride et al., _Conservation Science and Practice_ (2026): IRA fire rates not significantly different from roaded NFS land. The proposed rule concedes access raises human-caused ignitions. Caveat: Johnston et al. 2021 found IRAs had a larger share of area burned 1984 to 2018 with no severity difference; expect opponents to cite it. |

## Numbers used in the narrative, with sources

- Comments close 2026-09-21; docket FS-2025-0001: Federal Register, 91 FR 53827.
- 44.7 M acres; 37 states plus Puerto Rico; Alaska 14.8 M: USFS EDW 2001 IRA layer, summed.
- Tongass 16.7 M acres total, ~9.3 M roadless: USFS; EDW sum is 9,339,572.
- Juneau Icefield IRA 1,160,365 acres, the largest single polygon: EDW.
- Admiralty Island roughly 1,500 brown bears, with more bears than people: NASA Earth Observatory (2020) estimates roughly 1,500 bears; the National Park Service estimates more than 1,000 and confirms that bears outnumber people. The story uses the NASA estimate and the shared comparison rather than an unsupported exact human population.
- Rule history: >600 hearings, 1.6 M comments: Earthjustice timeline; 66 FR 3244.
- Tongass exemption 2020, restoration 2023: 85 FR 68688; 88 FR 5252.
- Prince of Wales logging history, Maybeso 1957 photo: USFS historical photo record.
- Dark Divide: largest unprotected roadless area in western Washington, 500-year trees: USFS Gifford Pinchot recreation page.
- BEA 2024 recreation figures: BEA release 2026-03-05.
- NVUM 158.7 M visits: 2022 National Visitor Use Monitoring Summary.
- Southeast Alaska jobs and wages: Southeast Conference, _By the Numbers 2025_.
- USDA revenue and recreation estimates and $6.9 B backlog: 91 FR 53829 to 53830. Road construction costs: Draft EIS Volume I, transportation-system analysis, reports $80,000–$100,000 per mile for native-surface roads, another $300,000–$500,000 per lane mile for gravel, and roughly $1–$2 million per lane mile for asphalt; Alaska costs are four to five times higher. The narrative conservatively summarizes that range as $80,000 to more than $2 million per mile.
- Tongass $44 M/yr loss: Taxpayers for Common Sense, *Cutting Our Losses* (2020), $1.73 B over 40 years.
- 4% federal share; labor constraints: RFF, Wibbenmeyer and Wear (2025).
- Canadian share ~24%; 450% increase needed: Fastmarkets (2025-05-15).
- ~45% combined duties and tariff: Commerce AD 20.56% + CVD 14.63% + Section 232 10%.
- Forest Service workforce down ~20% in 2025: GAO-26-107993.
- Ignition densities: Aplet et al. (2026), Table 2.
- 625,737 people and organizations; >99% opposed: 91 FR 53830; Center for Western Priorities (2025-09-19).
- Tribal opposition: 91 FR 53831.
- 55 members of Congress: Earthjustice press release (2025-09).
- Pew/Susquehanna poll, Dec 2025, n=1,200: 72% oppose repeal, 71% of Republicans.
- Colorado College 2026: 65% oppose industrial roads on undeveloped public lands.
- 25 M people, 9.5 M in California, 3.3 M in Colorado: Olden et al., _PLOS Water_ (2026).
- 327 threatened and endangered species: USDA DEIS.

## Possible follow-up datasets

These are enhancements rather than missing dependencies of the current story:

- Forest Service DEIS analysis layers on ArcGIS Online (Alt 1 IRAs, Alt 2 and 3 affected environment, half-mile road buffer): the authoritative "what is actually affected" geometry. Candidate for a follow-up chapter.
- NASA/USFS mature and old-growth probability maps (ORNL DAAC 2498, 100 m, CONUS only, Earthdata login): the best old-growth raster. Needs reprojection to a COG the viewer can read.
- ETH Global Canopy Height 2020 (10 m, CC BY): the Alaska gap-filler for canopy structure.
- Forests to Faucets 2.0 (HUC12 drinking-water importance): would let the "downstream" chapter show data instead of prose.
- USFS RoadCore roads and MTBS fire perimeters: for a roads-and-ignitions map.
